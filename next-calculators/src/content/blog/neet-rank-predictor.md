## Manual Calculation Methods (Formulas)

While the digital calculator handles the massive statistical mapping effortlessly, understanding exactly how the negative marking destroys your raw score is absolutely critical for your test-taking strategy inside the examination hall.

### The Raw Score Penalty Formula

The most dangerous trap in the NEET exam is "Blind Guessing." Because the penalty is -1, a string of bad guesses will literally erase the points you earned from correct answers.

**Raw Score = (Total Correct Answers x 4) - (Total Incorrect Answers x 1)**

**Example Equation:** You attempt 170 questions out of 180.
*   You get 140 correct answers. (140 x 4 = 560 Marks).
*   You wildly guess and get 30 wrong answers.
*   **The Penalty:** 30 x 1 = 30 Marks deducted.
*   **Final Raw Score:** 560 - 30 = **530 / 720**.

*(Notice the brutal reality: A score of 560 might secure a government seat in some states, but the 30 penalty marks crashed the score down to 530, permanently destroying the student's chances of free medical education).*

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is a "Safe Score" for a Government Medical College?**
Because of massive rank inflation, the definition of a "safe score" changes every year. For the 15% All India Quota (General Category), a raw score of 620+ (predicting an AIR under 20,000) is generally considered the absolute minimum safe boundary for a guaranteed government MBBS seat.

**2. Can a rank predictor be 100% accurate?**
No. A rank predictor is a highly sophisticated statistical estimate based on historical data and current-year paper difficulty. The exact performance of 2.5 million students is impossible to know until the NTA servers run the final algorithm. However, good calculators will provide a highly accurate "Range" (e.g., AIR 15,000 to 16,500).

**3. What happens if two students have the exact same raw score?**
This happens to thousands of students in the middle brackets. The NTA applies strict tie-breakers: (1) Higher marks in Biology, (2) Higher marks in Chemistry, (3) Higher marks in Physics, (4) Fewer incorrect answers across the entire test, (5) Older in age.

**4. How does the 85% State Quota work with the AIR?**
Every state must reserve 15% of its medical seats for the national pool (based purely on AIR). The remaining 85% of seats are reserved exclusively for students who physically live in that state. Your AIR is still used, but you only compete against the other students within your state, drastically lowering the required cutoff.

**5. What is the difference between MBBS, BDS, and BAMS?**
MBBS is Allopathic medicine (the highest cutoff). BDS is Dentistry (lower cutoff). BAMS/BHMS are Ayurvedic and Homeopathic medicine (significantly lower cutoffs). If your predicted rank fails the MBBS threshold, you can easily pivot your counseling strategy to secure a government seat in BDS or AYUSH.

**6. Does the difficulty of the paper affect the rank?**
Massively. If the Physics section is brutally difficult, the overall average score of the entire country drops. In this scenario, a raw score of 590 might suddenly secure a brilliant AIR of 12,000. If the paper is easy, 590 might crash to an AIR of 35,000. 

**7. Is negative marking really that dangerous?**
