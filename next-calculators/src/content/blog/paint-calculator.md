## Manual Calculation Methods (Formulas)

While the digital calculator handles the massive void deductions and multi-coat multiplication effortlessly, understanding the core coverage formula is an absolute mandatory skill for any homeowner negotiating with a paint supplier.

### The Standard Coverage Formula

This is the absolute law of paint estimation. You MUST deduct the voids before dividing by the spread rate.

**Total Liters = [ (Total Perimeter x Height) - Voids ] / Spread Rate**

**Example Equation:** You are painting a 20x20 foot square room with a 10-foot ceiling. You have one door (20 sq ft) and two windows (30 sq ft total). You are using a paint that covers 100 sq ft per liter for a single coat. You want 2 coats.
*   **Step 1 (Perimeter):** 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 80 linear feet.
*   **Step 2 (Gross Wall Area):** 80 x 10 = 800 Square Feet.
*   **Step 3 (Deduct Voids):** 800 - 50 (door + windows) = 750 Square Feet (Net Area).
*   **Step 4 (Single Coat Volume):** Divide 750 by the spread rate (100) = 7.5 Liters.
*   **Step 5 (Multiply by Coats):** 7.5 x 2 coats = 15 Liters.
*   **Final Answer:** You must physically purchase exactly **15 Liters of Paint**.

*(Notice the brutal reality: If you ignored the voids and just divided the 800 Gross Area by 100, you would calculate 8 liters per coat, or 16 liters total. You would have over-ordered by a full liter, wasting hundreds of rupees on custom-tinted paint you cannot return).*

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. How many coats of paint do I need?**
For a professional, long-lasting finish, you always need a minimum of **2 Coats**. The first coat bonds and creates the base color; the second coat provides the deep, uniform, opaque aesthetic finish. If you are painting a light color over a very dark wall, you may need 3 or 4 coats.

**2. What is a "Spread Rate" or "Coverage Rate"?**
The Spread Rate is a chemical measurement of how far 1 Liter (or 1 Gallon) of paint can be stretched across a wall before it becomes too thin to hide the color underneath. Premium paints have high spread rates; cheap paints have low spread rates.

**3. Why do I need to deduct doors and windows?**
Paint is highly expensive. A standard room might have a massive wooden door and two large windows, occupying over 50 square feet of wall space. Because these surfaces are either glass or polished wood (not painted with wall emulsion), you must deduct them from your total area to avoid buying excess paint.

**4. Do I calculate the ceiling with the walls?**
Only if you are painting the ceiling the exact same color as the walls. In modern interior design, the ceiling is almost always painted flat white to make the room feel taller. If the colors are different, you must calculate the ceiling volume completely separately from the wall volume.

**5. How much Primer do I need?**
Primer generally has a slightly lower spread rate than premium top-coat paint because it is designed to soak into the porous wall. A safe estimate is that 1 Liter of primer covers roughly 80 to 100 square feet for a single coat.

**6. Why is exterior paint calculated differently?**
Exterior walls are subjected to brutal weather. Exterior paint (like elastomeric weather-coats) is chemically formulated to be thick and rubbery to bridge hairline cracks and prevent water penetration. Furthermore, exterior plaster is usually very rough. This combination means exterior paints have a significantly lower spread rate, requiring much more volume per square foot.

**7. Can I use leftover interior paint on the outside of my house?**
Absolutely not. Interior paint lacks the heavy-duty UV blockers and waterproofing chemicals required to survive outdoors. The sun will bleach the color within weeks, and the first rainstorm will wash the interior paint right off the wall.
