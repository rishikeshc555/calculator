## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is a "Thappi" or Slab Block?**
When a massive cube of stone is pulled from a mountain, it is sliced vertically into 30 or 40 individual slabs at the factory. These slices are kept together in a stack (a Thappi). Slabs from the exact same stack will have identical colors and vein patterns.

**2. What does "Book-Matching" mean?**
Book-matching is a highly luxurious aesthetic technique where two consecutive slabs from the same block are placed side-by-side and opened like a book. Because they were sliced directly next to each other, their natural veins mirror each other perfectly, creating a beautiful, symmetrical butterfly pattern.

**3. Why do I need a 2-inch thick cement bed for marble?**
Ceramic tiles are perfectly flat and uniform. Raw stone slabs are slightly bowed, warped, and irregular in thickness. The mason needs a massive, thick bed of wet mortar to push and tap the heavy stone into, allowing them to perfectly level the uneven stone against the rest of the floor.

**4. Can I use grey cement to lay white Italian marble?**
No. White marble is highly porous and slightly translucent. If you use standard grey cement, the grey moisture will bleed through the stone and turn the beautiful white marble a permanent, dirty dull grey. You must paint the back of the slabs with expensive White Cement slurry before laying them.

**5. How is Granite different from Marble?**
Marble is a relatively soft, porous limestone derivative. It is beautiful but scratches and stains easily. Granite is an incredibly dense, brutally hard volcanic rock. It is virtually scratch-proof and highly resistant to acids, making it the perfect choice for heavy-duty kitchen countertops and high-traffic commercial floors.

**6. Why does marble need to be polished after installation?**
Unlike ceramic tiles which are fully finished in the factory, natural stone slabs are usually sold with a slightly rough, unpolished face. After the mason lays the floor, a specialized finishing crew must use heavy diamond grinding machines to flatten the entire floor (removing the tiny lips between slabs) and polish the stone until it looks like a mirror.

**7. How much waste should I calculate for a staircase?**
Staircases are incredibly complex and require massive amounts of edge-cutting, bullnosing (rounding the edges), and polishing. You should use a very aggressive waste factor (20% to 25%) when calculating the stone required for a staircase.
