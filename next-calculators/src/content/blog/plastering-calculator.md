## How does the Calculator work?

Because true plaster estimation relies on translating 2D square footage into incredibly thin 3D cubic volume—and then reversing that volume through a chemical shrinkage multiplier—the calculator requires strict algebraic sequencing.

The user wants to plaster an interior wall that is 10 meters long and 3 meters high, at a standard thickness of 12mm.
The calculator multiplies Length x Height to find the surface area (30 Square Meters).
It converts the 12mm thickness into meters (0.012m).
It multiplies the Area by the Thickness (30 x 0.012).

### Step 2: The Dry Shrinkage and Rebound Multiplier
The calculator knows you cannot buy 0.36 cubic meters of dry sand.
It applies a standard 30% to 35% multiplier to account for chemical water-shrinkage and physical waste (plaster falling off the trowel).
It multiplies the Wet Volume (0.36) by roughly 1.30.
It generates the absolute "Dry Volume" required for purchasing (0.468 Cubic Meters).

### Step 3: The Mix Ratio Split (Cement vs. Sand)
The user selects an economical 1:6 Mix Ratio (1 part cement, 6 parts sand; Total parts = 7).
The calculator divides the Dry Volume (0.468) by 7 to find the cement portion (0.0668 Cubic Meters).
It multiplies the cement volume by the strict physical density of standard Portland cement (1440 kg per cubic meter).
It divides the total kilograms by 50 to output the exact number of standard 50kg cement bags.
It allocates the remaining 6 parts to the sand, converting it into globally recognizable units (Cubic Feet or Cubic Meters).
It outputs the absolute, formatted architectural metric.

## Manual Calculation Methods (Formulas)

While the digital calculator handles the massive dry-shrinkage multipliers effortlessly, understanding the foundational formula for finding the wet volume is an absolute mandatory skill for any finishing contractor.

### The Standard Wet Volume Formula

This is the absolute law of plaster geometry. You MUST convert the millimeter thickness into standard meters or feet before multiplying.

**Wet Volume = Wall Area x (Thickness in mm / 1000)**

**Example Equation:** You are plastering an exterior wall that is 20 Square Meters at a thick 20mm depth.
*   **Step 1 (Unit Fix):** Convert 20mm to meters (20 / 1000 = 0.02 meters).
*   **Step 2 (Wet Volume):** Multiply the Area by Thickness (20 x 0.02). Volume = 0.4 Cubic Meters.
*   **Step 3 (Dry Multiplier):** Multiply the wet volume by 1.30 to account for shrinkage and waste (0.4 x 1.30 = 0.52 Dry Cubic Meters).
*   **Step 4 (The Mix Split):** If using a 1:4 ratio (5 total parts), divide 0.52 by 5 to find the cement volume (0.104 cubic meters).
*   **Final Answer:** You must multiply 0.104 by 1440 (cement density) and divide by 50kg. You need exactly **3 Bags of Cement** for this specific wall.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is the standard thickness of plaster for interior walls?**
The global architectural standard for interior, weather-protected partition walls is generally 12mm (roughly half an inch) thick.

**2. Why do exterior walls require thicker plaster?**
Exterior walls face brutal environmental stress (heavy monsoon rain, extreme heat, and wind). They generally require a 20mm to 25mm thick plaster (often applied in two coats) to ensure absolute waterproofing and structural defense.

**3. What does a 1:6 mix ratio mean?**
It is a volumetric recipe. 1:6 means for every 1 bucket of dry cement you pour into the mixture, you must pour in exactly 6 buckets of fine plastering sand.

**4. Why does the calculator multiply the wet volume by 1.30?**
When you mix dry cement and sand with water, the water fills the microscopic air voids between the particles, causing the entire mixture to physically shrink in volume by roughly 30%. To get 1 cubic meter of "wet" usable paste on the wall, you must physically purchase 1.30 cubic meters of dry raw materials.

**5. How much does a standard bag of cement weigh?**
In India and most parts of the world, a standard, commercially regulated bag of cement weighs exactly 50 Kilograms. The calculator relies on this standardized density to convert total required kilograms into an exact bag count.

**6. Why is the ceiling plaster ratio 1:3?**
Ceilings require an ultra-rich mixture of cement (1 part cement to only 3 parts sand) because the plaster must generate massive adhesive strength to fight gravity and bind permanently to the smooth underside of a concrete slab. 

**7. Can I use standard coarse river sand for plastering?**
No. You must use sieved, ultra-fine sand (often called Plastering Sand or Fine M-Sand). Coarse sand contains pebbles that will drag across the wall under the trowel, creating deep, ugly scratches in the finish.
