## Manual Calculation Methods (Formulas)

While the digital calculator handles massive decimal precision effortlessly, memorizing the core "rolling average" rule is an incredibly useful life skill when you are sitting in a counselor's office trying to figure out your current standing.

### The Standard Equal-Weight Formula

This is the most widely accepted conversion metric for quickly estimating your CGPA, assuming each semester has roughly the same total credit weight.

*   **Step 1:** Find your exact SGPA for every semester you have completed. (e.g., Sem 1: 7.0, Sem 2: 8.0, Sem 3: 9.0).
*   **Step 2:** Add all the SGPAs together. (7.0 + 8.0 + 9.0 = 24.0).
*   **Step 3:** Count the total number of semesters you just added. (In this case, 3).
*   **Step 4:** Divide the massive total by the number of semesters. (24.0 / 3).
*   **Final Answer:** Your official running CGPA is exactly **8.0**.

*(Notice the mathematical rescue: Even though the student started with a terrible 7.0, their later hard work perfectly balanced the equation, rescuing their cumulative score to a highly respectable 8.0).*

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is the difference between SGPA and CGPA?**
SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average) is your performance in one isolated 6-month block of time. CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is the massive, combined average of every single SGPA you have earned since you started your degree.

**2. Does a bad 1st semester permanently ruin my CGPA?**
Absolutely not. Because of the mathematical "Dilution Effect," a CGPA is highly malleable in the first two years. If you score a 6.0 in semester 1, but score 9.0s in the next three semesters, your overall CGPA will aggressively pull upward to an 8.25.

**3. Why do job portals only ask for CGPA?**
Corporate HR departments are processing thousands of resumes. They do not have the time to look at 8 different SGPA scores to figure out if you are a good student. They demand the single, unified CGPA metric because it represents your long-term consistency and reliability.
