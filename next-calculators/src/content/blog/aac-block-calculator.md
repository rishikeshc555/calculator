## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What does AAC stand for?**
AAC stands for Autoclaved Aerated Concrete. It is made using fly ash, cement, lime, gypsum, and an aluminum powder expansion agent, which is then cured under intense heat and pressure (in an autoclave), creating millions of tiny air bubbles.

**2. Why is a standard AAC block exactly 600mm long?**
600mm is a global architectural standard optimized for structural stability, weight distribution, and rapid laying speed by a single mason.

**3. Do I need to soak AAC blocks in water like red bricks?**
No! This is a massive mistake. AAC blocks should not be soaked. You only need to lightly sprinkle them with a wet brush to remove surface dust so the chemical adhesive can bond cleanly to the concrete.

**4. Can I use AAC blocks for load-bearing walls?**
While high-density AAC blocks exist, standard AAC blocks (which are highly aerated and lightweight) are almost exclusively used for non-load-bearing interior partition walls and exterior filler walls within a reinforced concrete (RCC) or steel frame.

**5. How much lighter is an AAC block compared to a red brick?**
An AAC block is approximately 3 to 4 times lighter than the equivalent volume of traditional solid red clay bricks. This massive weight reduction is why high-rise developers use them exclusively to lower structural dead-loads.

**6. Why is the adhesive sold in 40kg bags?**
40kg is the ergonomic standard for construction chemicals in many regions (like India), as it is the maximum safe weight for a single laborer to carry and mix on-site without heavy machinery.

**7. Do AAC walls require plastering?**
Because AAC walls are perfectly flat and machine-cut, they do not require thick, heavy coats of traditional cement plaster. They can be finished with a very thin 2mm to 3mm coat of specialized wall putty or gypsum plaster, saving even more money.
