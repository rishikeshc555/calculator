## How does the Calculator work?

Because true masonry estimation relies on calculating the difference between two separate volumes (the total wall versus the total clay), the calculator requires strict algebraic sequencing and a complex "dry-shrinkage" multiplier.

Here is exactly how the calculator processes your structural data safely behind the scenes:

### Step 1: The Matrix Volume (Finding the Brick Count)
If a user wants to build a 1-Cubic Meter wall (1m x 1m x 1m):
The calculator takes the standard Indian modular brick size (190mm x 90mm x 90mm).
It immediately adds the mandatory 10mm mortar joint to all sides, creating the "Nominal Size" (200mm x 100mm x 100mm).
It converts this to meters (0.2m x 0.1m x 0.1m) to find the volume of a single nominal brick (0.002 cubic meters).
It divides the total wall volume (1 cubic meter) by the nominal brick volume (0.002).
It outputs the absolute base brick count. (Output: You need exactly 500 Bricks to build 1 cubic meter of wall).

### Step 2: The Mortar Volume Deduction
The calculator knows you need 500 physical bricks. 
It calculates the actual volume of the raw clay without the mortar (500 x 0.19 x 0.09 x 0.09 = 0.7695 cubic meters of pure clay).
It subtracts the clay volume (0.7695) from the total wall volume (1.0).
It reveals the exact volume of the "Wet Mortar" joints (0.2305 cubic meters).

### Step 3: The Dry Volume Shrinkage Multiplier
This is the secret weapon of the calculator. When you mix water into dry cement and sand, the mixture physically shrinks by about 33%. Therefore, you cannot just buy 0.23 cubic meters of dry sand.
The calculator takes the Wet Mortar volume (0.2305) and multiplies it by a strict constant of 1.33.
It generates the "Dry Mortar Volume" required for purchasing (0.3065 cubic meters).

### Step 4: The Mix Ratio Split (Cement vs. Sand)
The user selects a 1:6 Mix Ratio (1 part cement, 6 parts sand; Total parts = 7).
The calculator divides the Dry Volume (0.3065) by 7 to find the cement portion.
It multiplies the cement volume by its massive physical density (1440 kg per cubic meter).
It divides the total kilograms by 50 to output the exact number of standard 50kg cement bags.
It allocates the remaining 6 parts to the sand, converting it into cubic feet.
It outputs the absolute, formatted architectural metric.

## Manual Calculation Methods (Formulas)

While the digital calculator handles the massive multi-tiered volume deductions effortlessly, understanding the foundational formula for finding the total brick count is an absolute mandatory skill for any contractor.

### The Standard Brick Count Formula

This is the absolute law of masonry geometry. You MUST add the 10mm mortar thickness to the brick before you do the division.

**Total Bricks = Total Volume of the Wall / Volume of 1 Brick WITH Mortar**

**Example Equation:** You are building a solid 10 Cubic Meter wall using standard Indian modular bricks (190 x 90 x 90mm).
*   **Step 1 (Add Mortar):** Increase the brick size by 10mm on all sides (200 x 100 x 100mm).
*   **Step 2 (Convert to Meters):** 0.2m x 0.1m x 0.1m.
*   **Step 3 (Volume of 1 Nominal Brick):** Multiply the dimensions. Volume = 0.002 Cubic Meters.
*   **Step 4 (The Division):** 10 Cubic Meters (Wall) / 0.002 Cubic Meters (Brick).
*   **Final Answer:** You need exactly **5,000 Bricks**. (Plus a 10% waste buffer, so you must order 5,500 Bricks).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is the standard size of a brick in India?**
The officially recognized "Modular Brick" standard by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) is exactly 190mm x 90mm x 90mm. However, many local traditional kilns still produce older "Non-Modular" bricks which are roughly 230mm x 110mm x 70mm (9 x 4.5 x 3 inches). You must measure your local bricks before calculating.

**2. Why do we add 10mm to the brick size in the calculation?**
Bricks cannot touch each other; they must be separated by mortar. The universal engineering standard for a masonry joint is 10mm (1 centimeter). To find out how much space a single brick actually occupies in the wall, you must add the 10mm mortar thickness to its physical dimensions.

**3. What does the 1:6 Mortar Ratio mean?**
It is a volumetric recipe. 1:6 means for every 1 bucket of dry cement you pour into the mixer, you must pour in exactly 6 buckets of sand.

**4. Why does the calculator multiply the mortar volume by 1.33?**
When you mix dry powder (cement and sand) with water, the water fills the microscopic air voids between the particles, causing the entire mixture to physically shrink in volume by roughly 33%. To get 1 cubic meter of "wet" usable paste, you must physically purchase 1.33 cubic meters of dry raw materials.

**5. How much does a standard bag of cement weigh?**
In India and most parts of the world, a standard, commercially regulated bag of cement weighs exactly 50 Kilograms. The calculator relies on this standardized weight to convert the total required kilograms of cement into an exact bag count.

**6. What is the difference between a 4.5-inch and a 9-inch wall?**
A 4.5-inch wall is built using a single layer of bricks laid lengthwise (known as a stretcher bond). It is only used for non-load-bearing interior partitions. A 9-inch wall is built using a double layer of bricks interwoven together. It is massively stronger, uses twice as many bricks, and is used for exterior load-bearing structures.

**7. Should I soak the bricks in water before building?**
Yes! Dry clay acts like a super-sponge. If you lay a dry brick on top of wet mortar, the brick will instantly suck all the moisture out of the cement, destroying the chemical curing process and ruining the structural bond. Always soak bricks in water before laying them.
