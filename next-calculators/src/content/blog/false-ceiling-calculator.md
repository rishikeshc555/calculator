Always demand that contractors build a hidden, removable access panel for all electrical drivers.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is the difference between Gypsum and POP?**
Gypsum comes in large, rigid, factory-manufactured boards that are screwed into a metal grid. It is fast, clean, and creates perfectly straight lines. Plaster of Paris (POP) is a wet paste applied by hand over a wire mesh. It is messy and slow, but it can be molded into any organic, curved, or circular 3D shape imaginable.

**2. Why do I need a false ceiling?**
Aside from the immense aesthetic luxury of hidden cove lighting, a false ceiling is functional. It acts as a massive shield that hides the ugly raw concrete slab, massive AC ducting, fire sprinklers, and chaotic electrical wiring required in modern homes and commercial offices.

**3. Does a false ceiling reduce the room temperature?**
Yes. A false ceiling creates a massive pocket of trapped air between the drop ceiling and the hot concrete roof. This trapped air acts as a fantastic thermal insulator, preventing the heat of the roof from entering the living space, significantly reducing your air conditioning bills.

**4. How is a false ceiling billed by contractors?**
Contractors universally bill false ceiling work by the **Square Foot**. This is why calculating the exact, mathematically honest surface area (including the vertical drops and deducting the empty voids) is the absolute most critical step in securing your budget.

**5. What is a "Cove" in a false ceiling?**
A cove is a structural ledge or recess built into the false ceiling. Its primary purpose is to hide a continuous strip of LED lights. The light bounces off the ceiling and spills into the room as a soft, luxurious, indirect glow, completely hiding the actual light source from view.

**6. Can a false ceiling hold a ceiling fan?**
The thin Gypsum board itself cannot hold any weight. The fan must be mounted to a long, heavy-duty steel hook (a fan box) that is drilled and anchored directly into the solid concrete roof *above* the false ceiling. The rod drops down through a small hole cut in the Gypsum board.

**7. Are false ceilings waterproof?**
Standard Gypsum and POP are highly vulnerable to water. If the roof leaks, the false ceiling will soak up the water, turn brown, sag, and eventually collapse. For bathrooms or areas prone to moisture, you must use specialized, highly expensive MR (Moisture Resistant) or PVC ceiling panels.
