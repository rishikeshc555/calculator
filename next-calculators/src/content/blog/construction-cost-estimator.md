## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is "Built-Up Area"?**
Built-up area is the total physical square footage of the roof slabs you are pouring. It includes the internal carpet area of the rooms, the space occupied by the thick brick walls, and the balconies. It is the absolute metric used to calculate construction cost.

**2. Why does "Luxury" quality cost so much more?**
The raw concrete shell (Grey Structure) costs roughly the same for a standard house and a luxury house. The massive price jump occurs during the finishing phase. A standard vitrified floor tile might cost ₹50 per sq ft. Imported Italian marble for a luxury build can cost ₹500 per sq ft. Smart-home wiring, heavy UPVC windows, and branded plumbing fittings exponentially multiply the budget.

**3. Does the calculator include the cost of buying the land?**
No. Construction estimators strictly calculate the cost of the physical building materials and labor. The cost of purchasing the raw dirt (the Plot) is entirely separate and must be negotiated based on highly volatile local real estate market rates.

**4. How much of my budget will go toward Cement and Steel?**
Combined, Cement (15-16%) and heavy TMT Steel reinforcement (20-25%) are the massive foundational pillars of your budget. Together, they will consume roughly 40% of your entire construction fund.

**5. Do I need to pay the contractor all at once?**
Never. Construction is paid in strictly defined "Milestone Phases." You pay a percentage when the foundation is dug, another percentage when the ground floor roof is poured, and so on. Never front-load money to a contractor, or they may abandon the site.

**6. Does the per-square-foot cost include the boundary wall?**
Usually, no. A standard contractor quote covers the main footprint of the house. Massive compound boundary walls, underground water sumps, and landscaping are considered "external development" and are usually billed as extra, separate line items.

**7. Can I use the calculator to apply for a bank loan?**
Yes. The detailed percentage breakdown provided by the calculator mimics a basic Bill of Quantities (BOQ). Bank loan officers require this exact type of segmented financial data to understand how you plan to spend the mortgage funds.
